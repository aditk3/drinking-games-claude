#### Overview

Good stories start with something you have never done. Get to know your friends, one confession at a time. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Nothing needed


#### Setup

1. Sit in a circle and agree on subjects everyone is comfortable discussing.
2. Choose a starting player; play moves clockwise. Decide on three full laps.


#### How to Play

1. The starter makes a true statement beginning “Never have I ever…” For example: “Never have I ever missed a flight.”
2. Everyone who HAS done that thing raises a hand. Sharing the story is optional. The speaker does not respond to their own true statement.
3. The next player invents a new statement. Anyone may pass or replace an uncomfortable prompt without explanation.


#### Drinking Rules

Players who have done the thing may take one optional small sip per statement, or simply raise a hand. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

Finish after three full laps, or whenever the group wants. There is no required winner.


#### Optional Variations

Ten Fingers: everyone starts with ten fingers up and lowers one for each applicable statement. First to zero ends the game; remaining fingers are the score. Try a five-finger mini game or themed prompts.


#### Four-Player Notes

With four, allow time for stories between statements. Avoid using prompts to single out one person.


#### Quick Start

Sit in a circle and agree on subjects everyone is comfortable discussing. The starter makes a true statement beginning “Never have I ever…” For example: “Never have I ever missed a flight.” Finish after three full laps, or whenever the group wants. There is no required winner.

#### Overview

Four friends, one question, and a very revealing vote. Find out how well your group really knows you. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Nothing needed


#### Setup

1. Sit where everyone can point at everyone else.
2. Take turns inventing lighthearted “Who is most likely to…?” questions; agree on ten prompts.


#### How to Play

1. The starting reader asks a question, such as “Who is most likely to adopt five dogs?”
2. Count down from three and point simultaneously. Everyone chooses one person; self-voting is allowed in this house version.
3. Count votes. The most-voted player gets one point. Tied players each get a point, with no repeat vote.
4. Rotate the reader clockwise and ask a new question.


#### Drinking Rules

At most one optional sip for a most-voted player, regardless of votes. Ties need no drinking penalty. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

After ten questions, the person with most points wins the unofficial superlative crown. Ties share the title.


#### Optional Variations

Use themes such as travel, cooking, or fictional survival. Play the minority-vote version or swap sips for harmless challenges.


#### Four-Player Notes

Four votes often tie; agree to share the result rather than escalating penalties.


#### Quick Start

Sit where everyone can point at everyone else. The starting reader asks a question, such as “Who is most likely to adopt five dogs?” After ten questions, the person with most points wins the unofficial superlative crown. Ties share the title.

#### Overview

A deceptively simple word game. Pick a category, keep the answers coming, and try not to draw a blank. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Nothing needed


#### Setup

1. Choose a starter and a category with many answers, such as countries, pasta shapes, or movie titles.
2. Agree on a five-second answer window and play five rounds.


#### How to Play

1. The starter names the category and gives the first valid example.
2. Proceed clockwise. Each player gives a new example within five seconds; repeats within that round are not allowed.
3. An invalid answer, repeated answer, or timeout ends the round. Settle disputed answers by group agreement; replay a disputed turn without penalty.
4. The player who ended the round chooses the next category and starts it. Record one loss for that player.


#### Drinking Rules

The player who loses a category may take one small sip at the round boundary, or take a point instead. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

Finish after five categories. Fewest lost rounds wins; ties share the win.


#### Optional Variations

Use a three-second timer, alphabetical answers, or a steady spoken beat. For example: France → Japan → Brazil → France ends the round.


#### Four-Player Notes

Rotate between broad and niche categories so one expert does not control every round.


#### Quick Start

Choose a starter and a category with many answers, such as countries, pasta shapes, or movie titles. The starter names the category and gives the first valid example. Finish after five categories. Fewest lost rounds wins; ties share the win.

#### Overview

Count to 21. Sounds easy? Wait until your friends start rewriting the rules. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Nothing needed


#### Setup

1. Sit in a circle; choose a starter and count clockwise.
2. House version: begin with normal counting. Add substitutions only after successful runs. Agree on five new rules maximum.


#### How to Play

1. Say one number per person, beginning at one, until someone reaches 21.
2. On 21, all say “Cheers to the Governor!” The player who said 21 replaces one number with a word or action, such as clapping instead of saying seven.
3. Restart at one with the next player. All previous replacement rules still apply.
4. A wrong number, forgotten action, or long hesitation resets the count to one with the next player. Do not erase the accumulated rules.


#### Drinking Rules

An error may cost one optional sip. Cap alcohol penalties at one per player per attempted count. Reaching 21 never requires drinking. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

Win cooperatively by completing a count with five custom rules, or stop after 20 minutes.


#### Optional Variations

Replace a number with a category answer, reverse direction at a number, or swap two numbers. Introduce one change at a time.


#### Four-Player Notes

The counting pattern repeats rapidly with four, so change the starter after every attempt.


#### Quick Start

Sit in a circle; choose a starter and count clockwise. Say one number per person, beginning at one, until someone reaches 21. Win cooperatively by completing a count with five custom rules, or stop after 20 minutes.

#### Overview

Two tiny phrases. One direction change. An unreasonable amount of tongue-tied laughter. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Nothing needed


#### Setup

1. Choose a starter and begin clockwise with the phrase “fuzzy duck.”
2. Agree on a three-second response window and eight rounds.


#### How to Play

1. The active player says “fuzzy duck” to pass play to the next person in the current direction.
2. Instead, they may say “does he?” This reverses direction and changes the active phrase to “ducky fuzz.” The previous player now acts.
3. Another “does he?” reverses direction again and changes the phrase back to “fuzzy duck.”
4. A wrong phrase, muddled pronunciation, or timeout ends the round. The player who made the error restarts clockwise with “fuzzy duck.”


#### Drinking Rules

One optional small sip at the end of a lost round. No doubling for accidentally saying something funny. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

After eight rounds, fewest errors wins. Count errors with fingers or paper if desired.


#### Optional Variations

Use a two-second response window or invent another reversal phrase. Decide whether immediate consecutive reversals are allowed; this version allows them.


#### Four-Player Notes

Track both the phrase and direction; the person who just spoke may immediately receive the turn back.


#### Quick Start

Choose a starter and begin clockwise with the phrase “fuzzy duck.” The active player says “fuzzy duck” to pass play to the next person in the current direction. After eight rounds, fewest errors wins. Count errors with fingers or paper if desired.

#### Overview

Keep the beat, watch the signs, and pass the call before your brain catches up. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Table


#### Setup

1. Sit around a sturdy table and give each player a distinct gesture, such as antlers or a salute.
2. Practice all four gestures. Choose a steady, comfortable table-tapping beat and a starter.


#### How to Play

1. Everyone taps the beat. The starter performs their own gesture, then another player’s gesture.
2. The called player responds on the next beat with their own gesture followed by somebody else’s.
3. Continue passing the call. Missing a call, using an unassigned sign, or losing the agreed beat ends the round.
4. Restart with the player who missed the call. Keep gestures unchanged until everyone is comfortable.


#### Drinking Rules

The player ending the round may take one optional sip between rounds. Never drink while trying to maintain the beat. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

Play eight rounds. Fewest missed calls wins; ties are fine.


#### Optional Variations

Gradually increase the tempo, use themed gestures, or play in two teams. The traditional introductory chant is optional.


#### Four-Player Notes

Use very different gestures at four players. A silent version works if table noise is unwelcome.


#### Quick Start

Sit around a sturdy table and give each player a distinct gesture, such as antlers or a salute. Everyone taps the beat. The starter performs their own gesture, then another player’s gesture. Play eight rounds. Fewest missed calls wins; ties are fine.

#### Setup

#### Setup

#### How to Play

#### What You Need

- Standard 52-card deck
- Empty cup or marker


#### Setup

1. Shuffle a standard deck and spread it face down around an empty central cup or marker. No shared drink is needed.
2. Choose a starter. Read the house rule map below aloud before drawing.


#### How to Play

1. Draw one card clockwise, announce its rank, and perform that rank’s action. Discard the card afterward.
2. Use this explicit low-intensity HOUSE MAP: **A:** last hand raised gets a point. **2:** ask another player a trivia question; incorrect gets a point. **3:** drawer gets a point. **4:** last to touch the floor gets a point.
3. **5:** drawer becomes thumb master until the next five; once, place a thumb on the table and last to copy gets a point. **6:** create one harmless table rule, expiring at the next six. **7:** last to raise a hand gets a point.
4. **8:** pick a teammate for the next mini-game, without duplicated penalties. **9:** start a clockwise rhyming chain; first repeat or five-second pause gets a point. **10:** play [Categories](#game/categories).
5. **J:** make one Never Have I Ever statement; applicable players get a point. **Q:** start a questions-only chain; an answer, repeat, or five-second pause gets a point. **K:** add one harmless rule; the fourth King ends the game.
6. A mini-game produces at most one point per player. A forgotten ongoing rule never stacks with another consequence from that card.


#### Drinking Rules

Optional house overlay: replace a point with at most one small sip, or keep all points. Traditional waterfalls, gender rules, shared drinks, and escalating quantities are replaced here. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

Stop at the fourth King or when the deck runs out. Fewest points wins. No one finishes the central cup.


#### Optional Variations

Kings and Ring of Fire are aliases. Use half a deck for a shorter session; swap any uncomfortable action for a compliment or charades.

#### Overview

Read the cards, clear the pyramid, and see who catches the last bus home. This guide uses a clearly defined four-player house version; regional rules may differ.


#### What You Need

- Standard 52-card deck


#### Setup

1. Choose a dealer. Shuffle 52 cards; keep Aces high, and agree that equal ranks lose prediction guesses.
2. House version: deal each player four cards through the guessing sequence, then build a 15-card pyramid in rows of 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 from the remaining deck.


#### How to Play

1. Starting left of the dealer, each player predicts red or black before their first card is revealed. Keep each revealed card in front of its owner.
2. For card two, guess higher or lower than card one. For card three, guess strictly inside or outside the ranks of the first two; equality loses. If the first two tie, only outside can win. For card four, guess a suit. Correct guesses earn a point.
3. Reveal the pyramid bottom row first, one card at a time. Any player holding the same rank may discard one matching card. There are no row multipliers or bluffing in this house version.
4. The player with most cards remaining rides the bus. Break ties with lowest prediction score, then a random draw.
5. Limited bus HOUSE RULE: that player makes four new red/black predictions from the remaining deck, recording successes. No resets; the bus ends after four cards.


#### Drinking Rules

Score prediction misses with tokens. Optional alcohol overlay: only the final rider may take one small sip after the bus. No repeated or escalating drinking. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

The bus ends after exactly four guesses. Fewest cards remaining wins the pyramid; the rider tries to earn at least two correct bus guesses.


#### Optional Variations

Skip the final bus or play pyramid only. Regional bus and pyramid layouts vary; the fixed-attempt version here deliberately replaces restart-based endings.


#### Four-Player Notes

Rotate the dealer for another session so everyone participates in setup.


#### Quick Start

Choose a dealer. Shuffle 52 cards; keep Aces high, and agree that equal ranks lose prediction guesses. Starting left of the dealer, each player predicts red or black before their first card is revealed. Keep each revealed card in front of its owner. The bus ends after exactly four guesses. 4. Wild Draw Four is legal only when you have no card matching the current color; the next player draws four and misses their turn. A challenge checks that legality: an illegal user draws four instead; a failed challenger draws six.
5. Say “UNO” as you play your second-to-last card. If caught before the next player begins, draw two. First to empty their hand wins the round.


#### Drinking Rules

Custom overlay: a player who draws because they cannot play may choose one small sip for that entire turn, independent of cards drawn. Alternative: only the highest remaining hand after a round may sip. Always allow points, a nonalcoholic drink, or a free pass.


#### How the Game Ends

For a quick session, play three rounds and count wins. Official long-form scoring awards opponents’ remaining cards to the winner (numbers face value, actions 20, Wilds 50), racing to 500.


#### Optional Variations

Play two partnerships with partners opposite. Blank Wild house rules depend on the owned edition; agree before dealing.


#### Four-Player Notes

Four is within classic UNO’s 2–10 range. Action cards affect only the next player unless the deck explicitly says otherwise.
